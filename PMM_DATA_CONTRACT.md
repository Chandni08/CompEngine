# Product Marketing data contract

This contract governs the Product Marketing (PMM) workspace. The implementation entry point is `buildMarketingWorkspaceModel()` in `app.js`; URL identity, row filtering, appendix consolidation, and KPI calculation live in `pmm-data-contract.js`. Every PMM renderer receives one filtered model so a KPI and its destination cannot derive the same concept differently.

## Core entities

| Entity | Definition and required fields | Derivation and unresolved-state rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence governance record | Every loaded evidence or signal record exposes `fieldCitable` (boolean) and `approvalState` (`draft`, `in-review`, `approved`, or `blocked`). | The browser ingestion gate derives `fieldCitable` conservatively from public-access, named-source, URL, link-health, source-control, language-type, evidence-status, and Waters-origin fields. It is `true` only when a named external public source has a confirmed working link and the record is safe to show a customer. Waters-only material, internal/analyst inference, unlinked records, unconfirmed or unsupported evidence, and unknown/broken links are always `false`. A missing or invalid approval state becomes `draft`; the gate never infers review or approval. |
| Product Comparator claim candidate | One exact, unchanged advantage statement extracted from a curated Waters-versus-competitor Product Comparator row. Required fields are `claimText`, `supportingEvidence`, `blockedEvidence`, `approvalState`, `status`, `fieldUsable`, and `studyRequiredBeforeFieldUse`. | `supportingEvidence` contains only explicitly linked records with `fieldCitable:true`. `status` is `supported` when at least one such record backs the exact wording and `gap` otherwise. A candidate can enter Claim Control only when it is supported and its approval state is not `blocked`; all gap candidates and approval-blocked candidates are routed to the shared `gapQueue` for Prompt 3. Vendor-only or single-sided specification records remain blocked evidence and cannot substantiate a relative advantage. |
| Governing position | Exactly one canonical PMM object containing target customer, priority segment, customer job, buying situation, reference class, primary value proposition, point of parity, point of difference, three evidence pillars, explicit exclusions, approval state/approver, and last-reviewed date. | Created once per filtered PMM workspace before decisions, claims, narratives, or assets. Its workflow frame is proposed analyst/rule-based inference: reliable, transferable, compliant analytical workflows rather than hardware specifications alone. With no approval record, its state is `Proposed — not approved`, approver is `Approver needed`, and review date is unresolved. |
| Market choice | One filtered 3Cs object (Customer, Company, Competition) plus a three-level competitor onion. Every alternative contains choice rationale, segment/buying situation, switching objection, proposed Waters response, required proof, confidence, classification, caveat, and exact source URLs where available. | Derived once from the Governing Position and existing customer, competitor, launch, comparison, and proof selectors before competitor-specific narratives. Form rivals include Agilent, Thermo Fisher, Shimadzu, and SCIEX when allowed by the global competitor filter, plus other competitors only when filtered exact public evidence exists. Category/resource and inertia alternatives remain visible even when evidence is missing so the strategic choice set is explicit. |
| Buying committee | A priority-segment decision unit containing bench user/analyst, method developer, QC/QA or validation veto, IT/data-integrity veto, lab-manager decider, procurement/economic buyer, and a conditional executive sponsor. Each role carries job, concern, modeled decision power, proposed message, required proof, objection, preferred asset/channel, classification, confidence, and exact role evidence. | The priority-segment working set is inherited from displayed evidence-backed positioning decisions, not record volume or an assertion of market attractiveness. A role is `observed` only when a matching exact customer-source URL is present. Role authority, message, channel, and committee relationships remain analyst/rule-based inference even when the role itself is observed. Missing roles remain visible as `Inferred role — validation required`. |
| Fishbein-style scorecard | Six buying attributes with segment-specific hypothesis weights, 1–5 Waters and competitor hypothesis scores, contextual evidence confidence, weighted difference, and a calculated swing attribute. | `normalizeHypothesisWeights()` guarantees weights total 100% per segment. `fishbeinScorecard()` calculates each weighted difference as `(weight / 100) × (Waters score − competitor score)` and selects the row with the largest absolute weighted difference, breaking ties by larger weight and then stable attribute order. Numeric weights and scores are never described as measured evidence until replaced by a governed study. |
| Adoption and Value Plan | One governed plan per displayed priority-segment working set. It contains all six ACCORD dimensions, a barrier inference and prescribed tactic for each dimension, three named EVC baseline classes, exact baseline/context links, editable low/base/high assumptions, and a value-claim gate. | Segment membership comes from the displayed positioning decisions, not record frequency. ACCORD barrier statements remain analyst/rule-based inference even when linked evidence provides context. EVC assumptions are stored in browser state by target + segment + competitor + baseline and are never converted into evidence or approval records. |
| EVC assumption range | One editable low/base/high range for a defined driver and unit: analyst time, method-transfer effort, validation effort, downtime, service burden, consumables, failed runs/rework, review time, training, or outsourcing. | `normalizeEvcRange()` accepts only finite numeric values and checks `low ≤ base ≤ high`. `buildEvcSensitivity()` aggregates only complete, ordered ranges and only within identical units. Hours, runs, and currency are never combined. Every initially blank value is labeled `Assumption — unsourced`. |
| EVC comparison baseline | The explicitly selected alternative against which the value model is framed: an incumbent Waters system, a named competitor, or keep-current-method/do-nothing. | Where an exact system/product is present in target-compatible evidence, its name and source are shown. Otherwise the baseline class remains named and the exact product is explicitly `unresolved`; no model name is fabricated. Changing the baseline selects a separate assumption set. |
| Value-claim gate | A deterministic control that prevents modeled or assumed economic value from entering the governed claims registry without claim-compatible substantiation. | `valueClaimEligibility()` returns registry eligibility only when substantiation is `Proven`. Commercial eligibility additionally requires established approval. Directional or Unsupported economics remain outside `marketingClaimsProofRows()` and export with `Blocked — substantiation required`. |
| Hierarchical target | One ordered selection: Market → Waters product → Application/workflow → Buying situation → Geography → Buyer role. | Market and geography reuse global filters. Waters product, application, situation, and buyer role are PMM-only selectors. Product options come only from the sourced `watersSystems` catalog. A product selection restricts competitor contexts to explicit product-comparison mappings, scopes Waters proof to the selected product, and adds a product/workflow compatibility requirement to governed claims; missing mappings or proof remain explicit empty or inapplicable states. Every change rebuilds one canonical workspace model, including governing adaptation, weights, competitor evidence-fit priority, proof, claims, narratives, actions, KPIs, break report, and exports. An incompatible child selection resets to `All`; it never carries silently into another market or product. |
| Positioning decision | A displayed PMM recommendation with audience/buyer, buying criterion or trigger, competitor narrative, proposed Waters counter-position, proof state, activation, confidence, recency, and exact source URLs. | Eligible when exact customer-language evidence supports the audience/criterion, or when an explicitly selected application/buying-situation target has an exact compatible competitor source and the audience is visibly marked as inference. Ranked by the documented rule-based score and limited to three. Missing candidates produce an empty state; the selector never pads with invented decisions. |
| Governed claim | A registry row containing exact proposed wording; claim type; segment/application; buyer; channel; baseline; exact evidence; unique-record and established-independent-source counts; comparability; substantiation; approval and approved wording; owner; expiration; and next action. | Built from the Governing Position plus a competitor-specific adaptation. Every evidence record is checked before it can contribute to substantiation. Proposed wording remains inference unless a separate approval record establishes otherwise. |
| Claim evidence compatibility | Six deterministic checks for product/workflow, attribute, segment/application, comparator, test conditions, and date/relevance. | A mismatch in any dimension makes that evidence `Inapplicable`. Missing metadata yields `Partially applicable`; it cannot yield Proven substantiation. Evidence is evaluated record by record so a rejected source remains visible without contributing support. |
| Evidence record | One source-backed input record before URL consolidation. | Record count is never reported as an independent-source count. Multiple records may point to the same URL. |
| Canonical evidence object | One appendix record per canonical HTTP(S) URL, identified by a deterministic `EV-XXXXXXXX` reference. It owns the full evidence summary, source metadata, consolidation note, and caveats. | Decisions, claims, committee roles, scorecard context, narratives, adoption plans, and artifacts display compact references to this object instead of repeating the proof narrative. Activating a reference expands the collapsed Evidence appendix and the containing evidence group, then focuses the canonical object. The identifier establishes identity and traceability only; it does not establish independence, compatibility, approval, or weight. |
| Independent source | A source published by an independently identifiable organization. | Organization independence requires an explicit source-organization identifier. It is not inferred from record count, URL, or domain. Current data does not consistently provide that identifier, so the UI reports unique URLs or source domains instead. |
| Source domain | The lowercase hostname of a canonical HTTP(S) URL, with a leading `www.` removed. | Used only for a diversity signal. A domain is not assumed to equal an independent organization. |
| Source family | Rule-based provenance class: Customer language, Waters official, Corporate filing, Conference or event, Scientific publication, Competitor official, or Other public source. | Derived from explicit type/source metadata and domain. It describes evidence provenance, not independence or quality. |
| Customer-language record | An observed public customer-language evidence record with an exact HTTP(S) URL. | Global filters apply before canonical URL deduplication. Forums can surface objections and language but are complaint-biased and not representative research. |
| Approval record | An explicit record establishing that Waters language is approved for use. | Technical proof never implies approval. No approval dataset is currently loaded, so applicable claims show `Approval not established.` and cannot be Ready. |
| Activation action | A PMM deliverable linked to a displayed positioning decision, including asset/action, audience, channel, owner, deadline, status, proof/approval need, measure, and evidence URLs. | One action is derived per displayed decision. Suggested fields remain suggestions; absent workflow data remains `Owner needed`, `Deadline needed`, or `Measure needed`. |
| Artifact-production record | One governed artifact template derived from the selected priority segment, active five-level target, Governing Position, buying committee, competitor narrative, claims registry, proof compatibility, objections, and exact evidence URLs. Supported records are competitive battlecard, positioning/messaging brief, regulated claims sheet, campaign/message plan, sales-deck outline, message-test brief, and customer-proof request brief. | Seven records are created for the selected segment. They reuse canonical selectors and never create new claims, approvals, owners, deadlines, customer outcomes, or proof. Changing any hierarchical filter rebuilds the records from the new workspace model. |
| Artifact workflow state | Editable owner, due date, production status, and success measure for one target + segment + competitor + artifact template. | Stored locally in browser storage under a versioned key. Blank owner, date, and measure remain visibly unresolved. Production status is explicitly separate from claims/legal approval and cannot change a claim's approval state. No backend assignment is implied. |
| Artifact export | A filter-specific PPTX, DOCX, or CSV representation of an artifact-production record or governed claims registry. | Battlecards and sales-deck outlines export to PPTX; briefs and claims sheets export to DOCX; the claims registry exports to CSV. Exact URLs remain hyperlink footnotes or source columns. Any output containing an unapproved claim is watermarked `DRAFT — NOT APPROVED`. Clipboard output includes approved wording only and is unavailable when no approved wording exists. |
| Start Here summary | One non-primary navigation summary derived from the same filtered workspace model. Required fields are chosen segment, governing-position status, calculated swing attribute, highest-risk visible claim, primary form-rival plus inertia threat, first ranked PMM decision, and nearest persisted artifact deadline. | It contains no independent business logic. Highest-risk claim uses the governed order Unsupported → Directional → Proven, then unresolved approval and inapplicable-proof penalties. The first decision is the first ranked positioning card. The nearest deadline is read only from persisted artifact workflow dates; absent dates display `Deadline needed`. |
| Segment filter | The global Market Segment value applied to records that contain a compatible segment/lab-type field. | `All Markets` applies no segment restriction. `Biopharma` includes only records coded to Biopharma by the existing filter utilities. Groups whose source schema lacks market/segment fields disclose that they do not respond to this filter. |
| What breaks? report | A pre-activation/export gate containing unsupported claims, inapplicable proof, missing observed buyer roles, missing economic assumptions, swing-attribute change from the comparable market baseline, and governing-position contradictions or unsupported deviations. | Calculated by `buildTargetingBreakReport()` from the same claims, committee, scorecard, and governing traces used by the rendered workspace. Empty categories say so explicitly. Break states are retained in the export and never converted into approval or proof. |
| Confidence score | A 0–100 average of the numeric confidence values on the exact evidence inputs used by the row/card. | Missing numeric inputs produce `Confidence unresolved`. Confidence does not establish approval, commercial importance, source independence, or proof sufficiency. |

## URL identity and deduplication

A canonical URL is a valid HTTP(S) URL after lowercasing the hostname, removing a leading `www.`, removing the fragment, removing a trailing path slash, deleting `utm_*`, `fbclid`, and `gclid` parameters, and sorting remaining query parameters. A canonical URL is counted once across the Evidence appendix, even when several evidence records refer to it. The first displayed appendix group owns the consolidated entry; its note reports how many underlying records shared the URL. Each linked entry receives one deterministic `EV-XXXXXXXX` identifier used by downstream references. Unlinked records remain visible as unresolved evidence but are excluded from URL KPIs.

## Evidence governance gate

`evidence-governance.js` runs immediately after the engine loads its JSON datasets and current link-health snapshot. The gate enriches records without removing or renaming existing fields, so Product Management rendering continues to consume the same data shapes. Product Marketing then applies the `fieldCitable` and `approvalState` filters before signals, launches, application notes, customer records, filings, conference evidence, historical evidence, technical evidence, source coverage, claims, narratives, or artifacts enter `buildMarketingWorkspaceModel()`.

- `fieldCitable` is derived, never promoted by record volume, confidence, Waters authorship, or an approval-state value.
- `approvalState` defaults to `draft`; only an explicit valid state is preserved.
- `approved` does not make an otherwise unsafe record field-citable.
- `fieldCitable:true` does not imply Waters legal/claims approval.
- Link health must be confirmed `ok`/`good` in the current link-health data or explicitly healthy on the record. Unknown health is not treated as working.
- The two governance filters are Product Marketing controls and do not alter Product Management rendering.

`product-comparator-claim-transformer.js` reads every displayed Waters comparator strength, explicit curated differentiation statement, and explicit Waters-favorable technical comparison sentence, preserving the source wording exactly. It does not infer support from keyword similarity: a backing record must be embedded with the advantage or explicitly reference the deterministic candidate ID or exact claim text. Current Product Comparator spec syntheses backed only by vendor records therefore remain gaps. Claim Control renders only `fieldUsable:true` candidates in the exact-wording, applicable-proof, blocked-evidence, approval-state, and study-required columns; gap wording is never presented as field-usable.

## KPI definitions

| KPI | Precise calculation | Unit | Filters and destination |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Positioning decisions | `eligible positioning candidates → rule-based rank → first 3 → length` | Displayed cards | All global filters apply. Claims-matrix filters do not. Destination: Positioning decisions; the count equals `.pmm-positioning-decision` cards. |
| Claims without approval | `visible governed claim rows where approvalEstablished = false → length` | Displayed rows | All global filters and substantiation/audience/classification registry filters apply. Destination: Claims at Risk; the count equals displayed registry rows lacking a legal/claims approval record. |
| Exact customer sources | `filtered customer-language records → valid canonical URLs → unique → length` | Unique URLs | All global filters apply. Destination: Segment and Buying-Committee Cascade, which states the same full filtered URL count; exact records remain in the Customer-Language Records appendix group. This is not a count of independent organizations. |
| Direct evidence sources | `all applicable appendix records → valid linked canonical URLs → unique across all groups → length` | Unique URLs | Global filters apply where source fields exist. Historical product/capability records intentionally ignore horizon, geography, and market when their schema lacks those fields. Destination: Evidence appendix summary; unlinked records are excluded. This is not a count of records or independent organizations. |

Each KPI link has an `aria-describedby` tooltip containing its definition, calculation, unit, and filter behavior. The tooltip appears on pointer hover and keyboard focus.

## Claims-registry invariants

- `Proven` requires fully compatible evidence and at least two explicitly identified independent source organizations.
- `Directional` means some relevant evidence exists, but compatibility metadata or independent corroboration is incomplete.
- `Unsupported` means no applicable supporting evidence remains after compatibility checks.
- `Ready` requires `Proven` substantiation and an established legal/claims approval record. Every other combination is `Blocked`.
- A unique URL is a unique evidence record, not an independent source organization. Independent corroboration requires explicit organization identity and independence metadata.
- Inapplicable evidence remains linked for auditability but contributes nothing to substantiation.
- A negative, neutral, or mixed customer record is a concern or criterion, never a competitor strength.
- A source-domain count is never labeled an independent-source count.
- No duplicate canonical URL contributes more than one to Exact customer sources or Direct evidence sources.
- Every downstream Waters counter-position, claim response, and activation asset carries the governing-position identifier and inherits its unapproved state.
- Market Choice never reports alternative prevalence. Record, URL, and domain counts describe evidence coverage only.
- A form-rival choice rationale is classified as observed only when a positive customer-source record with an exact URL supports it. Negative, neutral, or mixed records remain traceable objections and cannot become reasons to choose the rival.
- Regulatory records may support compliance context elsewhere in the PMM workspace, but they are excluded from direct customer-choice evidence and cannot make a Market Choice alternative observed.
- `Outsource testing`, `Use a CRO/CDMO`, and any other alternative without direct customer-choice or win/loss evidence are classified `Strategic hypothesis requiring validation`, not observed demand.
- `Do nothing / keep the validated method` is always represented as an inertia competitor. It becomes observed only when a filtered customer record directly supports method-continuity, legacy, replacement-timing, or upgrade-friction behavior.
- Every Market Choice Waters response inherits `Proposed — not approved`; public product or technical proof does not change its approval state.
- Buying-committee inclusion does not establish role authority. Decision power is a rule-based working model until confirmed by segment-specific primary research.
- Fishbein weights always total 100% but remain hypotheses. Numeric Waters and competitor scores remain `Hypothesis — validation required`; linked records provide context and do not validate the number.
- Application, buying-situation, and buyer-role adjustments to Fishbein weights are deterministic analyst hypotheses. Geography can trigger a rebuild but does not change a weight without geography-specific preference evidence.
- The Governing Position references the selected segment's calculated swing attribute, its hypothesis weight, and weighted difference. The swing cannot be manually declared.
- Claims evidence must match market segment and application/workflow as separate compatibility dimensions when a hierarchical application is selected. Matching the broad market alone cannot substantiate an application-level claim.
- Competitor priority under hierarchical targeting is evidence-fit priority based on recency, source-domain diversity, source-family diversity, and scored confidence. When a Waters product is selected, a competitor must also have an explicit comparison mapping to that product; no technology-based comparator is silently substituted. It is not market attractiveness, prevalence, win rate, or commercial priority.
- The exported PMM snapshot contains the active six-level target and its break report. Unsupported claims and inapplicable proof remain explicit records and cannot inherit a prior target's support state.
- Replacing a weight or score requires a dated win/loss study, survey, or conjoint analysis with method, segment, respondent role, sample, owner, review state, and expiration state.
- Every priority segment exposes relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, observability, risk, and trialability. A linked source may contextualize a barrier but does not make the barrier prevalence observed.
- Every ACCORD barrier has a prescribed launch tactic and evidence gate. Controlled evaluation, method migration, risk reversal, reference sites, validation packages, comparative proof, onboarding, and proof-of-value milestones remain proposed tactics until ownership and approval records exist.
- EVC range totals are unit-specific sensitivity summaries, not a monetary value claim. A monetary EVC remains unavailable until conversion rates, implementation cost, price/discount, currency, utilization, time horizon, and claim-compatible outcome evidence are established.
- Sourced baseline links and unsourced numeric assumptions are displayed in separate containers. User-entered assumptions never become sourced evidence.
- A value claim cannot enter the claims registry unless its substantiation status is `Proven`; it cannot become commercially Ready unless approval is also established.
- Artifact production consumes the governed claims registry; it never writes a modeled or proposed value statement back into that registry.
- Artifact workflow status (`Draft`, `Blocked`, `Ready for review`, or `Complete`) is operational metadata only. It cannot imply legal/claims approval.
- An artifact is treated as approved only when it contains at least one claim and every included claim has both `approvalEstablished = true` and non-empty approved wording. Otherwise its UI and exported file carry `DRAFT — NOT APPROVED`.
- PPTX and DOCX evidence footnotes preserve exact HTTP(S) URLs. CSV protects formula-like cell values and stores URLs in a dedicated evidence column.
- Clipboard copying filters to exact approved wording. Proposed wording, inferred counter-positions, and unapproved text are never copied by the approved-text control.
- Repeated proof summaries and caveats are owned by one canonical appendix object. Downstream objects may display its identifier, role in the decision, compatibility status, and a concise caveat count, but must link back to the canonical object for the full detail.
- Local competitor adaptations are checked against the governing customer job, value proposition, point of difference, and exclusions. Comparative superlatives or guarantees are contradictions; adaptations with no workflow-value overlap are unsupported deviations. Both states are visibly flagged and cannot be styled as approved.

## Reconciliation audit

Before this contract, the All Markets UI showed 4 claims awaiting approval while the matrix contained 14 legally unapproved rows; 27 exact customer sources while the appendix contained 22 unique customer-language URLs; and 242 direct evidence sources while the appendix contained 514 records/links (397 unique URLs). The sidebar also counted three positioning decisions independently of the filtered cards.

The corrected implementation calculates all four KPIs from the same `buildMarketingWorkspaceModel()` arrays passed to their destination renderers. All Markets and Biopharma are covered by contract tests, including duplicate-URL fixtures and assertions that the KPI equals the destination collection length.
